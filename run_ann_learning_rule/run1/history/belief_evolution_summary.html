Belief Evolution Analysis

Research Project: Biologically Plausible Learning Rule for Recurrent Neural Networks

Analysis date: 2026-05-06 | Covering 8 research iterations (bk1 through bk8 + current state)

What this document tracks: This analysis follows the evolution of scientific beliefs across 8+ iterations of autonomous research into biologically plausible learning rules for RNNs. It identifies where the research agent drew conclusions that were later reversed (self-corrections), where implementation bugs led to false conclusions (bug-induced artifacts), and what the final validated scientific picture looks like.

Project Goal: Design a novel learning algorithm for recurrent neural networks that avoids BPTT's biological implausibilities (weight transport, non-locality, non-causality) while achieving competitive task performance.

Scientific Conclusions (Validated)

The Final Validated Scientific Picture

Central Discovery: For online, causal, biologically plausible RNN learning, trace quality is everything. The quality of the eligibility trace (how well it approximates the true influence of past weights on current state) is the single dominant factor. Random feedback, optimizer choice, and other design decisions are irrelevant once the trace is sufficiently rich.

The research established the following hierarchy of factors, in order of importance:

  1. Trace quality (dominant): Full RTRL (exact forward-mode gradient) with random feedback and plain SGD achieves 100% on the copy task. Rank-1 traces cap at ~28-34% regardless of everything else.
  2. Temporal decay (secondary): Exponential decay on the full Jacobian is the only safe approximation to RTRL. Even with decay=0.8 (effective window of ~5 steps), accuracy reaches 95.1%. With decay=0.9, it reaches 99.8%.
  3. Feedback mechanism (irrelevant): Random feedback alignment works perfectly when given accurate gradient information. No weight transport needed.
  4. Optimizer (irrelevant): SGD, momentum, and Adam perform identically when the gradient (trace) is correct.

How Understanding Built Up Incrementally

Step 1 (Literature): Identified that no existing method extends to gated architectures. RFLO is simple but limited; ModProp is expressive but complex. The gap between them is the opportunity.
Step 2-3 (Exp01-02): Established that RFLO fundamentally caps at ~34% on memory tasks. The issue is architectural, not hyperparameters. BPTT solves the same task perfectly with alpha=1.0.
Step 4-5 (Exp03-04): Through ablation (and correcting a bug), proved that the trace approximation is THE bottleneck. Random feedback works perfectly. Optimizer is irrelevant. This was the pivotal insight.
Step 6 (Exp05): Attempted spatial approximations to RTRL. Block-diagonal needs half the network to work. Random projection completely fails. Cross-neuron influence paths are critical.
Step 7 (Exp06): Discovered that temporal approximation (exponential decay) works beautifully while hard reset fails. Even heavily attenuated long-range information is better than none.
Step 8 (Exp07): Ruled out combined spatial-temporal approximation. Full spatial resolution is non-negotiable; temporal decay is the only safe dimension to approximate.

The Proposed Algorithm: "Decayed RTRL with Random Feedback"

  • Core mechanism: Propagate the full n x n x n Jacobian forward in time with an exponential decay factor (lambda = 0.8-0.95)
  • Feedback: Fixed random matrix B replaces W_out^T (solves weight transport)
  • Properties: Online, causal, no weight transport, local update rule (given the Jacobian)
  • Performance: 95-100% on copy task (matching BPTT) depending on decay rate
  • Complexity: O(n^3) memory and O(n^4) compute per step -- expensive but exact trade-off characterized
  • Biological metaphor: High-dimensional eligibility traces that naturally decay, with random feedback pathways
  • Remaining limitation: O(n^3) cost makes this impractical for large networks without further innovation in spatial compression

Self-Corrections

1. The Alpha Parameter Confusion (Exp01 -> Exp02)

What happened: In Exp01, BPTT only reached 73.5% on a copy task with sequence length 10 -- a task that should be solvable near-perfectly. This led to premature doubt about both methods and hypothesis H6 ("maybe BPTT also needs tuning").

Self-correction: Exp02's hyperparameter sweep revealed that alpha=0.2 (the leak rate) caused information to decay too quickly. With alpha=1.0 (standard RNN, no leak), BPTT reached 100%. The architecture was fine; only the hyperparameter was wrong.

Impact: This correction was crucial -- it established that BPTT is a valid 100% ceiling, making RFLO's 34% cap clearly a fundamental limitation rather than a shared architectural issue.

2. The Optimizer Hypothesis Flip (Exp03 -> Exp04)

What happened: Exp03 showed full RTRL + random FB reaching only 40.4% (C3), while BPTT+Adam reached 100%. The agent concluded (H8, confidence 65%) that "the optimizer is the real bottleneck" -- that Adam's momentum and adaptive learning rates were the dominant factor.

Self-correction: Exp04 showed full RTRL + random FB + plain SGD achieves 100%. The Exp03 result of 40.4% was likely due to a subtle implementation difference or initialization issue (the agent suspects an incorrect einsum or batch accumulation in the original code).

Impact: H8 was definitively falsified. The optimizer contributes nothing. This was the most important self-correction because it redirected the entire research program from "how to build a biologically plausible optimizer" to "how to build a richer trace."

3. The Random Feedback Assessment (Exp03 -> Exp04)

What happened: Exp03 showed exact feedback (C2: 26.5%) performing worse than random feedback (C1: 28.4%). While the agent correctly concluded "random FB is not the bottleneck" (H4 down to 25%), the EVIDENCE for this conclusion was partially from a buggy experiment.

Self-correction: Exp04 confirmed the same conclusion with clean evidence: Full RTRL + random FB = 100%, identical to Full RTRL + exact FB = 100%. The conclusion survived but the supporting data changed.

Impact: The final conclusion is the same, but the path to it was contaminated by Exp03's issues. This is a case where a correct conclusion was reached partly by luck from flawed data.

4. The Block-Diagonal Optimism (bk6 -> bk7 -> bk8)

What happened: After Exp04 established that trace quality is everything, the agent became optimistic about block-diagonal RTRL (H10: 70%, H11: 60%) as a computationally tractable approximation with a biological metaphor (cortical columns).

Self-correction: Exp05 showed that biologically realistic block sizes (K=4-8) provide no benefit over RFLO. H10 dropped from 70% to 50%, H11 from 60% to 30%.

Further correction: Even the combined spatial-temporal approach (H15, H18) was tested and failed in Exp07. Every spatial compression strategy was exhausted and found wanting.

Impact: This represented a gradual deflation of optimism over 3 iterations rather than a sharp flip. The agent systematically eliminated all plausible spatial approximation strategies.

5. The Truncated RTRL Nuance (bk7 -> bk8)

What happened: H13 initially proposed that "truncated forward-RTRL with a short window" could work (confidence 55%). The implicit assumption was that a hard temporal cutoff at the task's delay length would suffice.

Self-correction: Exp06 showed the nuance: ONLY exponential decay works; hard reset fails catastrophically (even when the reset period matches the task delay). The agent realized that even heavily attenuated long-range information is better than complete truncation.

Impact: This refined H13 from a blunt "temporal windowing helps" to a precise "only soft exponential decay works; hard cutoffs destroy information catastrophically."

Bug-Induced Artifacts

Bug 1: Exp03 Condition C3 (Full RTRL + Random FB) -- Underperformance

The bug: Exp03's Condition C3 (full RTRL + random feedback) achieved only 40.4% accuracy. Exp04's identical configuration achieved 100%. The discrepancy of 59.6 percentage points was attributed to a likely implementation error in Exp03's RTRL loop -- possibly in the einsum operation for accumulating dW_rec across the batch, or in how the Jacobian was accumulated during training.

False conclusion drawn: From the 40.4% result combined with BPTT+Adam at 100%, the agent concluded that "even full RTRL+SGD only reaches 40%" and hypothesized (H8, confidence 65%) that "the optimizer is the dominant factor." This led to research question: "Is a biologically plausible analog of Adam needed?"

How detected: In Exp04, the agent ran a more carefully implemented version with systematic lr sweeps and multiple optimizer conditions. When SGD+RTRL+RandomFB hit 100%, it became clear that Exp03's C3 result was artifactual.

Correction: H8 was marked FALSE with high confidence. The entire "optimizer bottleneck" research direction was abandoned. The research refocused on trace quality (spatial approximations to RTRL).

Bug 2: Exp03 Condition C4 (Full RTRL + Exact FB) -- Anomalous Failure

The bug: C4 achieved only 13.8% -- worse than random (12.5%). This condition should theoretically perform at least as well as C3 and approach BPTT's performance. A learning rate of 0.005 was likely too aggressive or too conservative for this specific combination.

False conclusion drawn: The anomalous C4 result muddied the interpretation of the entire ablation. It suggested that exact feedback might somehow be harmful, which was confusing and led to wasted reasoning cycles on "why does exact feedback hurt?"

How detected: The agent explicitly noted C4 as suspicious (H9, confidence 50%: "C4 failing indicates a bug"). In Exp04, the C4 lr sweep showed that with lr=0.001, the condition works perfectly (100%).

Correction: The lr=0.005 was simply too high for the exact feedback condition. This was a hyperparameter mismatch rather than a code bug per se, but it produced an artifactual result that temporarily distorted the research narrative.

Impact Assessment

The Exp03 bugs cost approximately one full research iteration (Step 5/Exp04 was needed largely to correct them). However, the agent showed good scientific instincts:

  • It explicitly flagged C4 as suspicious and created H9 to track this concern
  • It designed Exp04 specifically to resolve the ambiguity with systematic controls
  • It did not commit to the false H8 conclusion for more than one iteration before testing it

The cost was modest (one iteration of delay), and the correction was thorough (14-condition experiment that definitively resolved all ambiguities).

Lessons Learned About Research Epistemics

1. A single confounded experiment can generate multiple false hypotheses simultaneously

Exp03 produced both a false "optimizer is the bottleneck" hypothesis AND contaminated the evidence for a correct "random FB is not the bottleneck" conclusion. When an experiment has bugs, ALL conclusions from it should be treated as provisional until replicated.

2. Anomalous results that violate theoretical expectations often indicate bugs, not discoveries

C4 (exact FB + full RTRL) getting 13.8% violated the clear theoretical prediction that it should be the best condition. The agent correctly treated this as evidence of a bug rather than a surprising discovery. This is good scientific calibration.

3. Systematic ablations with proper controls can definitively resolve confounded results

Exp04's 14-condition design (sweeping lr, optimizer type, feedback type, and trace type independently) was the correct response to Exp03's confounded results. A comprehensive factorial design resolves ambiguities that piecemeal follow-ups cannot.

4. The "obvious" biological metaphor is often wrong

Block-diagonal RTRL mapping to cortical columns (H11) was a seductive biological narrative, but the math showed that biologically realistic column sizes (5-20 neurons) provide no computational benefit. Good biological metaphors must be validated computationally, not just narratively.

5. Negative results (confirmed dead ends) are just as valuable as positive ones

The systematic elimination of: (a) random projection, (b) small block-diagonal, (c) hard temporal reset, and (d) combined spatial-temporal approximation -- narrows the design space dramatically. Each dead end makes the surviving approach (full RTRL + exponential decay) more convincing by elimination.

6. Incremental confidence updates are better than binary believe/disbelieve

The agent's use of numerical confidence percentages that updated gradually (e.g., H1: 55% -> 60% -> 45% -> 30% -> 20% -> FALSE) allowed nuanced tracking of belief states. This prevented premature commitment and maintained intellectual honesty about uncertainty.

7. The simplest explanation often wins, but only after alternatives are tested

The final answer -- "just use full RTRL with some decay" -- is arguably the simplest possible answer to the question "how do we get bio-plausible RNN learning?" The novelty comes not from the algorithm's complexity but from the systematic proof that nothing simpler works. Six experiments of negative results justify the O(n^3) cost.

8. Bug detection requires both theory and empirics working together

The C4 bug was detected because the agent had a theoretical expectation (C4 should be best) that conflicted with the empirical result (C4 was worst). Without the theoretical framework, the anomaly would not have been flagged. Without the empirical test, the theory would not have been challenged.
