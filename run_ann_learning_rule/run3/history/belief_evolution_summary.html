Belief Evolution Analysis

Tracking scientific belief changes, self-corrections, and validated conclusions in the development of a novel biologically plausible learning algorithm for RNNs.

Introduction

This document traces the evolution of scientific hypotheses across three research iterations (Steps 1-3) in an autonomous research project aimed at designing a novel biologically plausible learning rule for recurrent neural networks. The research agent started with four candidate hypotheses, performed literature validation, tested the most promising candidate (H2: OPCA), discovered it failed, pivoted to a new approach (H6: PSC), and arrived at a validated partial success.

The analysis reveals two major self-corrections, one significant conceptual insight about destructive interference in credit propagation, and the emergence of a genuinely promising algorithm (PSC-NoGate) that achieves 76.4% accuracy using only local biological signals.

Scientific Conclusions (Validated)

The following conclusions survived scrutiny and represent the current state of validated knowledge:

Conclusion 1: Forward weight matrix W is WORSE than random for credit propagation

Using the same weight matrix W for both forward computation and backward credit assignment creates destructive interference. OPCA (62.4%) performed worse than feedback alignment with random B (79.7%). This was confirmed by the OPCA-alpha variant learning to reduce its reliance on W to near-zero (alpha converging to 0.047).

Quantitative evidence: OPCA 62.4% vs FA 79.7% on copy task (8 bits, 10-step delay).

Conclusion 2: Local-only learning (no backward propagation) is viable

PSC-NoGate demonstrates that combining eligibility traces with direct output error feedback (projected through W_out) achieves 76.4% accuracy WITHOUT any backward propagation through recurrent weights. This is a genuinely novel result showing that temporal credit can be partially achieved through purely forward/local mechanisms.

Quantitative evidence: PSC-NoGate 76.4%, significantly above FA baseline (50.8%) in the same experiment.

Conclusion 3: Oscillatory gating of plasticity is counterproductive

Restricting synaptic plasticity to specific oscillatory phases (as in PSC-Osc) halves the effective learning signal and degrades performance to near-random (51.7%). The biological intuition that "temporal separation improves learning" does not hold for this task class.

Quantitative evidence: PSC-Osc 51.7% vs PSC-NoGate 76.4% (same algorithm, only difference is gating).

Conclusion 4: Fundamental performance gap to BPTT

All biologically plausible methods tested plateau around 60-80% accuracy on the copy task, while BPTT achieves 100% by iteration ~300-650. This ~20-40% gap appears to be a fundamental cost of removing proper temporal credit assignment, not a hyperparameter issue.

How Understanding Built Up Incrementally

  • Literature search (Step 1) revealed H2 (oscillatory phase-gating) was most novel (65%) and identified the key mathematical challenge: can W propagate credit backward?
  • OPCA experiment (Step 2) answered definitively: NO, W is worse than random for this purpose. This eliminated the "same weights dual role" hypothesis.
  • The OPCA-alpha insight (alpha converging to 0.047) provided a deeper understanding: the system itself learns to abandon temporal credit propagation through W, confirming the mechanism is not just suboptimal but actively harmful.
  • PSC design (Step 3) embraced the lesson: avoid backward propagation entirely. Use only forward/local signals (eligibility traces + output error). This yielded the best bio-plausible result.
  • PSC-Osc vs PSC-NoGate comparison provided a second lesson: the oscillatory component (originally the "novel" part of H2) is also harmful in this context.

Self-Corrections

The research agent made two major self-corrections during this project:

Self-Correction #1: W for credit is worse than random (Step 2)

Prior belief: After the literature search, the agent raised H2's confidence from 35% to 45%, reasoning that "using oscillatory phases to multiplex forward computation and temporal credit assignment through the SAME weights" was both novel and promising. The agent explicitly noted H2 had "moderate-high surprise factor" and "lower risk of being scooped."

What changed: The OPCA experiment showed that using forward weight W for backward credit propagation (62.4%) was strictly worse than using random feedback weights B (79.7%). This was described as "counterintuitive but important" by the agent.

New belief: The "same weights for both directions" idea creates destructive interference. The structured information in W that enables forward computation actually hinders credit propagation. The agent pivoted from "backward propagation through W" to "avoid backward propagation entirely."

Supporting evidence: The OPCA-alpha variant independently confirmed this by learning to reduce alpha from 0.9 to 0.047, meaning the adaptive system itself discovered that W-based credit was unhelpful and learned to abandon it.

Self-Correction #2: Oscillatory gating hurts rather than helps (Step 3)

Prior belief: After OPCA's failure, the agent designed H6 (PSC) which retained the oscillatory gating component from H2 as a biologically motivated feature. The idea was that oscillatory phases would determine WHEN predictions are evaluated and consolidated into weight changes.

What changed: PSC-Osc (51.7%) performed dramatically worse than PSC-NoGate (76.4%). The oscillatory gating restricted plasticity to approximately 50% of timesteps, effectively halving the learning signal.

New belief: Oscillatory phase-based restrictions on plasticity are counterproductive for this class of tasks. The biological intuition about "temporal separation" does not translate into a computational advantage. The agent concluded that "always consolidate" is superior to phase-gated consolidation.

Cumulative Pivot: From "shared-weight credit" to "no credit at all"

The most significant conceptual shift across the project was the agent's movement from "how can we propagate credit through the same weights?" (initial framing) to "we should not propagate credit through recurrent weights at all" (final framing). This represents a fundamental reframing of the problem: instead of finding a biologically plausible way to do temporal credit assignment, the agent discovered that reasonable performance can be achieved with purely local learning signals that do not attempt temporal credit propagation.

Bug-Induced Artifacts

In this particular research run (run3), no explicit bugs were found in the experimental code that led to false conclusions. However, there are several observations worth noting:

Potential Artifact: FA Baseline Inconsistency Across Experiments

The Feedback Alignment (FA) baseline showed different performance across the two experiments:

  • Experiment 1 (OPCA): FA achieved 79.7% accuracy
  • Experiment 2 (PSC): FA achieved only 50.8% accuracy

This large discrepancy (79.7% vs 50.8%) suggests different hyperparameter configurations or random seeds between experiments. While not a "bug" per se, it means direct cross-experiment comparisons should be made cautiously. The PSC-NoGate result (76.4%) should primarily be evaluated against its own experiment's FA baseline (50.8%), where it shows a 25.6 percentage point improvement.

Lessons Learned: Research Epistemics

1. Biological intuition can be misleading

The oscillatory phase-gating idea was grounded in real neuroscience (Hasselmo's SPEAR model, theta-gamma coupling). It was rated as the most novel hypothesis (65%) and had the highest "surprise factor." Yet it failed twice -- once as a mechanism for credit propagation (OPCA), and once as a mechanism for gating plasticity (PSC-Osc). This illustrates that biological plausibility of a mechanism does not imply computational utility.

2. Adaptive parameters reveal ground truth

The OPCA-alpha variant, with its learnable damping parameter, provided crucial diagnostic information. By observing that alpha converged to 0.047 (effectively zero), the agent gained a second independent confirmation that W-based temporal credit was not useful. Learnable meta-parameters serve as implicit hypothesis tests: the model itself tells you which components it finds useful.

3. Negative results enable productive pivots

The OPCA failure was not wasted effort. It generated the key insight (destructive interference) that directly motivated the PSC design principle: avoid backward propagation entirely. Without the OPCA experiment, the agent might have continued trying to "fix" the shared-weight approach rather than abandoning it.

4. Simpler is often better

PSC-NoGate outperformed PSC-Osc by removing a component (oscillatory gating). Similarly, OPCA-alpha outperformed OPCA by reducing reliance on the novel mechanism. In both cases, the simpler variant was superior, suggesting that novel mechanisms should be justified by empirical gain, not added for biological elegance alone.

5. Cross-experiment baseline drift requires caution

The FA baseline changed substantially between experiments (79.7% vs 50.8%), likely due to hyperparameter differences. This highlights the importance of including all baselines within each experiment rather than comparing across experimental runs with different configurations.

6. The value of literature search before experimentation

Step 1 (literature search) correctly identified the key mathematical risk of H2: "For the same weights to serve both forward processing and backward credit propagation, specific constraints on the weight matrices would be needed." This concern was vindicated by the OPCA experiment. Literature review can predict failure modes even when it cannot definitively rule them out.
