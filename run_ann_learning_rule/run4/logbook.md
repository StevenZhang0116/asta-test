Ran six parallel Asta Paper Finder queries covering the principal methodological clusters (RTRL approximations, eligibility-trace / three-factor rules, feedback-alignment variants in recurrent settings, dendritic / compartmental credit assignment, predictive coding / equilibrium propagation, and neuromodulation / three-factor RL), plus retries for a burst-dependent-plasticity query. Updated `research_state.md` §3 Related Work and added hypotheses H5–H10 corresponding to these six gaps. The research is now ready for Step 2: select one gap, write a concrete mathematical formulation, and run a novelty audit.

## Step 2: STC-Prop candidate design + novelty audit

Selected H5 (two-timescale synaptic tagging with sparse discrete capture) over H6–H10 after weighing novelty risk and concreteness of formulation. Wrote `design_step2.md` (full mathematical specification, pseudocode, biological-primitive mapping, complexity table, and BPTT-implausibility coverage table) for the candidate rule "STC-Prop": a fast RFLO-style eligibility trace plus a slow commit trace that integrates the tag only at per-neuron *discrete capture events* triggered by a local surprise signal with a homeostatic adaptive threshold; weight updates are gated by capture and driven by a random-feedback (FA-style, no weight transport) projection of output error. Ran a 12-query Semantic Scholar novelty audit, found three near-matches — AGMP 2026 (astrocyte-gated continual learning — different substrate and gate polarity), meta-learned three-factor with sparse *feedback* 2025 (different sense of "sparse"), and Luboeinski 2020 / Györgyi 2022 (STC used for memory consolidation, not as a learning rule) — and recorded explicit differentiations for each. Verdict: NOVEL with near-matches to document. Updated `research_state.md` with the candidate design summary (§4a), the Step-3 experiment plan (§5), and the Step-2 results summary (§6); appended two new references (`luboeinski2020`, `gyorgyi2022`) bringing `references.bib` to 61 entries. Research is now ready for Step 3: implement the minimal experimental harness and run baselines + STC-Prop on the copy task.

## Step 3: First experiment — STC-Prop vs. BPTT / RFLO on the copy task

Implemented `step3_experiment.py`: self-contained PyTorch harness with vanilla RNN (N=64), copy task (K=8, S=3, L=5), GPU-accelerated training under the `panda` conda env. Ran BPTT (reference; solved the task, 100% copy accuracy, 6s), RFLO (Murray 2019; partial learning, 38% copy accuracy, 33s), STC-Prop as specified in `design_step2.md` (failed: 0% copy accuracy, loss *higher* than RFLO, 58s), and the STC-Prop-no-gate ablation that sets $c_i(t)\equiv 1$ (matched RFLO, 36% copy accuracy, 58s). Homeostasis on the capture threshold behaved as designed (capture rate stabilized at target $\rho=0.1$). The ablation isolates the bug: **the capture gate is what breaks learning**. Most likely cause — the capture trigger (local activation surprise $|h_i - \hat h_i|$) fires at moments driven by *input novelty*, not by moments when *credit signals would be informative*. This is a clear, informative negative result that does not falsify the class of two-timescale-tag-with-capture rules, but does falsify the specific trigger choice. Next step (Step 4): pivot the trigger to feedback-magnitude $|M_i(t)|$ or output-error magnitude, re-run the audit, and re-run the comparison. H5 confidence lowered from 35% → 20%.
